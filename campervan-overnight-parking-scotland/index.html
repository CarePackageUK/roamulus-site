Overnight parking in a campervan in Scotland

What Scotland’s access rules do and do not allow for campervans and motorhomes, where you can stay overnight, and how to leave no trace.

This is a plain-English summary to help you plan, not legal advice. Rules differ by place and can change, so check the official sources at the end and any signs where you stop.

The short version

  • Scotland’s right of responsible access lets people wild camp in tents. It does not cover motor vehicles.
  • Sleeping in a parked campervan or motorhome is not wild camping under the Scottish Outdoor Access Code.
  • Stopping overnight at the roadside is not a right. Where it happens, it has relied on the goodwill of landowners.
  • A rooftop tent counts as part of the vehicle, not as a tent.

Where you can stay

  • Campsites and touring parks. The simplest option, with water, waste disposal and often showers and electric hook-up.
  • Designated stopovers. Some car parks and sites allow overnight stays for self-contained vans, sometimes for a small fee. Look for signs that say so.
  • With permission. If you want to stop on private land, ask the landowner first.

Where not to stop

  • Anywhere signed “no overnight parking” or with other parking restrictions.
  • Passing places on single-track roads, gateways, driveways and field entrances.
  • Areas with their own local rules. Parts of Loch Lomond & The Trossachs National Park, for example, have seasonal camping byelaws; check the park’s own guidance before you go.

Leave it better than you found it

  • Empty toilet cassettes and grey water only at proper disposal points, never outdoors or into drains.
  • Take all rubbish with you if the bins are full.
  • Arrive late, leave early, keep noise down, and do not light fires on the ground.
