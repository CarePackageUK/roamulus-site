North Coast 500 by campervan: a 7-day itinerary

A day-by-day outline for driving Scotland’s North Coast 500 in a campervan, with how long to allow, the Bealach na Bà warning and single-track road tips.

The North Coast 500 is a loop of about 516 miles that starts and finishes in Inverness. This outline is a starting point, not a timetable: check road conditions, closures and campsite availability before you go.

How long to allow

The distance is fixed; the pace is yours. Much of the west and north is single-track road, so miles take longer than they do on a main road.

Days on the routeAverage per dayWhat it feels like
5 daysAbout 103 milesMostly driving, short stops
7 daysAbout 74 milesA drive and one proper stop each day
10 daysAbout 52 milesTime for walks, beaches and a rest day

A seven-day outline, clockwise

  1. Inverness to the Applecross peninsula. Head west through Lochcarron. See the note on the Bealach na Bà below before you choose your road.
  2. Applecross to Torridon and Gairloch. Coast and mountain scenery in Wester Ross.
  3. Gairloch to Ullapool. A shorter day, with a town at the end for fuel, food and water.
  4. Ullapool to Durness, through Assynt. Lochinver sits on the way. This is one of the slowest and most dramatic stretches.
  5. Durness to Thurso, along the north coast. Tongue is a natural halfway stop.
  6. Thurso to John o’ Groats and down the east coast. Wick, then on toward Helmsdale and Golspie.
  7. Dornoch and the Black Isle, back to Inverness.

Less confident on narrow roads? Drive it anti-clockwise, so the easier east coast comes first and the hardest roads come once you have settled in.

The Bealach na Bà

The pass over to Applecross is steep and winding. The route’s organisers say it is not suitable for larger motorhomes, caravans or inexperienced drivers, and they point those drivers to alternative roads. It can also be closed in winter. If your van is large, or you are in any doubt, take the alternative.

Driving single-track roads

  • Use passing places to let oncoming traffic through, and to let faster vehicles behind you overtake.
  • Never park in a passing place.
  • Fill up with fuel and water when you can; stations and taps are far apart in the north-west.
  • Book campsites ahead in summer. See our guide to overnight parking in Scotland for what is and is not allowed outside a campsite.
